# RECOURSE: Urban Intersection Redesign for Safety and Efficiency

### The Asymmetric Median Flare Strategy | Nirmaan'26 — Case Study 04

**Team Code:** N26-035  
**Domain:** Transportation Engineering · Traffic Signal Design · Road Safety · Geometric Design

## Overview

RECOURSE is a conceptual redesign of a congested four-leg signalised urban intersection, developed to improve traffic operations and pedestrian safety within an existing right-of-way (ROW), without additional land acquisition.

The proposed design combines asymmetric median flaring, revised lane allocation, signal-phase optimization, pedestrian refuge provision, and night-time visibility interventions.

The central design challenge is to balance traffic capacity, pedestrian protection, geometric constraints, and operational feasibility within a fixed roadway cross-section.

## Problem Statement

The existing intersection configuration presents five principal challenges:

- High traffic demand and potential oversaturation under the assessed flow conditions.
- Unprotected right-turn movements that create conflicts with pedestrians.
- Long pedestrian crossing distances without a dedicated refuge island.
- Limited space for geometric improvements because of the zero-land-acquisition constraint.
- Inadequate pedestrian visibility and protection at night.

The project evaluates an alternative intersection layout and signal strategy intended to address these issues together rather than treating congestion and pedestrian safety independently.

## Project Objectives

1. Evaluate the existing intersection geometry and traffic-flow conditions.
2. Develop a geometric redesign within the stated 18 m roadway cross-section.
3. Assess revised signal timing and protected right-turn operation.
4. Introduce a pedestrian refuge and additional crossing-safety measures.
5. Compare the existing and proposed concepts using traffic, geometric, and pedestrian-safety criteria.

## Proposed Design

### 1. Asymmetric Median Flare

The proposed cross-section reallocates the existing 18 m roadway width to provide:

| Component | Proposed width |
|---|---:|
| Incoming carriageway | 9.0 m |
| Raised pedestrian refuge median | 2.0 m |
| Outgoing carriageway | 7.0 m |
| **Total** | **18.0 m** |

The proposed incoming approach provides three 3.0 m lanes, while the outgoing carriageway retains two 3.5 m lanes.

The asymmetric arrangement is intended to provide additional incoming approach capacity while accommodating a pedestrian refuge without outward widening.

### 2. Traffic Signal Optimization

The design evaluates Webster's signal-timing method using the assumed design flows, saturation flows, and lost times documented in the project presentation.

The proposed concept introduces three signal phases:

- East–west through movement.
- North–south through movement.
- Protected right-turn movements.

The presentation proposes a nominal cycle length of 96 seconds, compared with the stated existing cycle of 110 seconds. These values are proposed design parameters and require reconciliation with the detailed signal-timing calculations before being treated as validated operational results.

### 3. Pedestrian Safety

The proposed pedestrian-safety measures include:

- A 2.0 m raised refuge island for staged crossing.
- Protected right-turn operation.
- Setback stop lines to manage heavy-vehicle encroachment.
- Yellow-box junction markings.
- Raised table-top pedestrian crossings.
- Kerb ramps and visible road markings.

### 4. Night-Time Safety

The proposed concept includes a combination of:

- Photoluminescent concrete.
- Solar-powered LED road studs.
- High-visibility thermoplastic road markings.
- High-mast LED lighting.

These measures are proposed design interventions; their field effectiveness and photometric performance have not been established through implementation in this case study.

## Preliminary Design Comparison

The presentation reports the following existing-versus-proposed design values.

| Parameter | Existing concept | Proposed concept |
|---|---:|---:|
| Signal cycle length | 110 s | 96 s |
| Total flow ratio, Y | 0.973 | 0.758 |
| Reported volume-to-capacity ratio | 1.050 | 0.867 |
| Signal phases | 2 | 3 |
| Raised pedestrian refuge | Not provided | 2.0 m |
| Total roadway cross-section | 18.0 m | 18.0 m |
| Additional ROW requirement | — | None proposed |

**Important:** These are preliminary values reported in the competition presentation. They are not independently validated traffic-performance measurements. The reported flow ratios, capacity calculations, cycle lengths, and pedestrian timing require consistency checks before the table can be interpreted as a verified performance comparison.

## Methodology

The project follows a conceptual engineering workflow:

1. Review the existing intersection configuration and stated traffic demand.
2. Calculate design flow rates using the assumed peak-hour factor.
3. Assess critical flow ratios and preliminary signal-timing requirements.
4. Develop an asymmetric median flare and revised lane allocation.
5. Evaluate signal phasing and pedestrian crossing requirements.
6. Identify geometric and operational constraints.
7. Compare the proposed concept with the existing configuration.
8. Review relevant Indian Roads Congress (IRC) publications cited in the presentation.

## Limitations

- This is a conceptual design and case study, not a construction-ready engineering drawing package.
- Traffic demand, peak-hour factors, saturation flows, and other input assumptions require verification against field data.
- Signal timing and capacity results require independent recalculation and reconciliation.
- Pedestrian clearance, refuge dimensions, turning paths, and heavy-vehicle swept paths require detailed geometric checks.
- IRC compliance claims must be verified against the applicable official standards.
- Proposed lighting and visibility measures require appropriate photometric and field validation.
- No post-implementation traffic or crash-reduction results are available from this conceptual study.
